# Narrative Standards & Writing Guide

This document defines the thematic, stylistic, and mechanical standards for the narrative portfolio of **Kim Jin-Young** (operating as **Velvet**). 

Our primary standard of quality is professional short speculative fiction. We aim for the stylistic density, restraint, and precision of *The New Yorker* or *Clarkesworld*, using the dystopian lens of Shadowrun 6th Edition to critique the idol industry, examine the erasure of personal identity, and explore the cost of achieving autonomy.

---

## 1. Core Thematic Pillars

Every narrative chapter or log entry must actively engage with at least one of these thematic pillars. Magic and technology in these stories are never just aesthetic dressing—they are literal manifestations of commodification and control.

### I. The Manufactured Commodity
*   **The Concept:** The corporate entertainment complex does not sign talent; it manufactures products. A child is bought, bio-sculpted, and trained to be a specific consumer asset.
*   **Shadowrun Integration:** Jin-Young's features were not just styled; they were surgically and magically erased. His pan-Asian "Universal Face" is corporate intellectual property, mathematically optimized to maximize consumer engagement across multiple regions.
*   **Narrative Focus:** Highlight the dissonance between the character's internal, fragmented sense of self and the flawless exterior designed to be consumed by millions.
*   **R&D Constraints (The Clinical Horrors of Optimization):** Treat cutting-edge recursive neural networks and ancient, infernal entities not as mythic wonders, but as sub-contracted R&D vendors on a corporate spreadsheet (e.g., AI loops running forty thousand iterations for left eyelid density, bound spirits with humming corporate compliance collars refining jawlines to align with succubus paradigms). Maintain cold, clinical restraint; the horror must lie in the mundane, legalistic, and bloodless nature of the exploitation.

### II. Magic as a Corporate Extractive Industry
*   **The Concept:** In the corporate world, spirituality, emotion, and magical potential are natural resources to be harvested, refined, and fed into the grid.
*   **Shadowrun Integration:** The stadium concerts of TXM (The Death Messengers) were massive Geomantic Rituals. The emotional high of 80,000 screaming fans was funneled through Jin-Young’s unique Dalakitnon astral architecture, acting as a power conduit to manipulate local ley lines and corporate markets.
*   **Narrative Focus:** Magic is physically exhausting, invasive, and carries a profound cost. Describe spellcasting and rituals as draining, clinical, and sometimes violent exchanges, rather than effortless high-fantasy displays.

### III. The Performative Trap (The Illusion of Autonomy)
*   **The Concept:** The most insidious trap of the idol industry is the performance of emotion—love, rivalry, rebellion—under corporate choreography.
*   **Shadowrun Integration:** Jin-Young's forbidden relationship with Hana ("Luna") from Yueying began under the lens of a Matrix variety show, where their interaction was algorithmically structured and edited. Even their genuine moments were haunted by their handlers' surveillance.
*   **Narrative Focus:** Examine the difficulty of finding real human connection when every gesture, expression, and word has been trained, sculpted, and monitored. 

### IV. The Cost of Agency & Rebellion
*   **The Concept:** Escaping the corporate machine is not a clean, heroic triumph. It is a messy, destructive rupture that leaves scars and collateral damage.
*   **Shadowrun Integration:** Velvet's sabotage of the Singapore mega-ritual saved Hana's life but shattered the lives and careers of his bandmates, who now hunt him. He lives in Seattle's rain-slicked sprawl, selling his skills to covertly fund Hana's safety.
*   **Narrative Focus:** The lingering guilt of leaving others behind, the paranoia of being hunted, and the irony of using his corporate training (weaponized charisma, the "Universal Face") to survive the shadows.

## 2. Stylistic Directives (The "New Yorker" Standard)

To maintain a high literary bar, we reject standard pulp sci-fi tropes, action-movie dialogue, and expository info-dumps.

### I. Emotional Understatement & Restraint
*   **Rule:** Avoid loud, melodramatic outbursts, heroic monologues, or characters shouting at the void. Trust the reader to detect internal conflict through action and sensory detail.
*   **Example (Pulp Cliché):** *"I won't let them control me anymore!" he screamed, casting a massive fireball that lit up the Seattle rain.*
*   **Example (Literary Restraint):** *He kept his palm pressed flat against the concrete of the alley until the spell took. The drain hit him behind the eyes—a dry, chemical ache that tasted of burnt copper. He did not look back at the security sedan.*

### II. Sensory and Technical Realism
*   **Rule:** Ground the sci-fi and magical elements in visceral, physical reality. Magic should feel heavy, clinical, and dangerous. Cyberware, biotech, and bio-sculpting should feel intrusive, sanitary, and physically demanding. Shape-shifting is not a magical trick of light, but a painful, biological reorganization of cartilage, muscle, and digital signatures.
*   **Example (Pulp Cliché):** *She was highly cybered and moved like a lightning bolt, her cyber-eyes glowing neon red. He waved his hand and used his magic to instantly turn into a female elf to trick the guard.*
*   **Example (Literary Restraint):** 
    *   *When Hana turned her head, there was a minute, high-frequency hum from the actuators in her cervical vertebrae. Her eyes—perfect, glassy spheres of Wuxing-patented optics—did not blink, but instead shuttered with a faint click as she adjusted the exposure to the dim light of the green room.*
    *   *He sat on the lid of the stained porcelain toilet, the Transys Avalon humming against his thigh as it re-keyed the commlink to broadcast Lee Ji-yoo. In the mirror, the localized tissue-sculpting fought the absence of the Mitsuhama blockers, the cartilage in his trachea settling with a wet, heavy click that dropped his vocal range. He gripped the edges of the sink until his knuckles turned the color of bone, waiting for the target blueprint symmetry to slide back over his jaw.*

### III. Microscopic Economy of Prose
*   **Rule:** Every sentence must be actively chiseled. Avoid filler words, repetitive possessives, and redundant modifiers. If a word or detail does not contribute to character depth, theme, or atmosphere, remove it.

### IV. Linguistic Identity & The Trauma of the Shift (The Pronoun Rule)
*   **The Rule:** Pronouns and linguistic demeanor are strictly locked to his active biological form. When configured as Lee Ji-yoo, the text must use she/her, and her demeanor must adapt to the soft, algorithmically optimized elegance mandated by the label. When configured as Tanaka Ryo, the text must use he/him, maintaining a sharp, masculine baseline tempered by the clean, effeminate symmetry of the idol industry.  
*   **The Charisma 12 Standard:** A Charisma of 6 is the maximum possible for a human. An 8 is borderline supernatural. Velvet's Charisma rating of 12 is an unearthly, supernatural presence. The prose must illustrate this physical perfection—unblemished facial symmetry, the hypnotic cadence of their voice, the precise weight of their posture—without ever sliding into pulp romance clichés or provocative smut. Their beauty should feel slightly uncanny, a hyper-optimized corporate design meant to disarm the viewer.  
*   **The Toll of Transition:** Shifting forms is a visceral violation of the body. The prose must document the bone-deep ache of resetting cartilage, the shifting of muscle mass, and the chemical nausea of re-keying localized DNA strands. The internal monologue must reflect the psychological friction of this displacement—the vertigo of a self that is constantly being overwritten.  
*   **Example (Literary Execution):** 
    > She adjusted the lapels of the silk jacket, her fingers tracing the flawless line of a collarbone that had belonged to someone else an hour ago. The restructuring had left a lingering, metallic heat in the back of her throat, her lungs still adapting to the shortened volume of her new ribcage. As Ji-yoo, her voice carried a clear, silver chime—a neurologically mapped pitch designed to lower the heart rate of anyone listening. She smiled at the mirror, waiting for the cybernetic irises to finish stabilizing over the deep, bottomless obsidian of her blood.

### V. The Corporate Sovereign (The Threat of Extreme Presence)
*   **The Scale:** Charisma 12 is an unworldly, catastrophic gravity. It is the pinnacle of what late-stage capitalism can engineer when unrestricted by ethics or budget. It must never be written as a superficial "pretty face." It is a physical and psychological force that disarms the cynical, silences boardrooms, and commands an unholy, obsessive adoration from the most powerful executives in the Sixth World.  
*   **The Strategic Vulnerability:** This level of perfection is impossible to mask in polite society. Any corporate executive, high-tier fixer, or intelligence asset will immediately recognize the distinct, staggeringly expensive hallmark of an elite megacorporate asset program. When Velvet operates as Velvet, Ji-yoo, or Ryo, their presence acts as an economic signature. It doesn't just charm the mark; it signals that a runaway asset of unimaginable value is walking the streets, actively re-igniting the corporate hunger to reclaim the investment.  
*   **The Narrative Pacing of the Trap:** The realization that Velvet's protective network has been anticipated, and that Hana's rise is a gilded honeytrap, must hit him as a crushing twist during his Seattle operations (rather than him suspecting it from the beginning). This maximizes the dramatic irony of his struggle, turning his secret shadowruns and payments into the very trace mechanics that seal his compliance.

## 3. The Two-Stage Review Framework

Every new draft or revision session must proceed through these two distinct stages of review, executed proactively:

* **Workflow Rule:** The agent should analyze the target text using the Stage 1 and Stage 2 prompts, formulate the improvements, and immediately apply the changes. The user will review, accept, or discuss specific revisions.

### Stage 1: Thematic Centering

* **Review Prompt:**
  > *"As a fellow expert in TTRPGs and character design, I want your honest review. Tell me what you think the central theme of the piece should be. Help me identify what is working well to support the theme, and what (if anything) is getting in the way."*
* **Thematic Alignment:**
  1. Does the scene reinforce the commodification of the character or the cold reality of the corporate machine?
  2. Are magical or cybernetic elements treated as visceral and demanding, rather than generic power-ups?
  3. Is the conflict rooted in the psychological toll of the character's past and his ongoing search for autonomy?

### Stage 2: Quality Benchmarking (The New Yorker Standard)

* **Review Prompt:**
  > *"Act as an elite literary fiction editor and an uncompromising critic of speculative fiction. I am going to share a draft of a story with you. I want you to evaluate it with absolute rigor, holding me to the highest possible standard of craft. Do not pull your punches or offer polite encouragement; point directly to where the prose needs a chisel.
  > 
  > Evaluate and critique the text using these specific criteria:
  > 
  > 1. **THE SCALE**: Rate the story on a 1-to-10 scale, where 1 is a competent high school student's fanfiction and 10 is a transcendent, genre-subverting piece that *The New Yorker*, *Clarkesworld*, or an elite literary anthology would happily publish.
  > 
  > 2. **NARRATIVE SCAFFOLDING**: Identify where the story leans on safe, well-traveled genre tropes or predictable emotional resolutions. Challenge me to introduce psychological complexity, bittersweet ironies, or genuine existential messiness.
  > 
  > 3. **SHOWING VS. MONOLOGUE**: Catch any moments where the narrative voice breaks character to explain its own subtext, summarize an internal realization, or use abstract technical vocabulary to describe a visceral sensory experience.
  > 
  > 4. **MICROSCOPIC ECONOMY**: Scrutinize the text at the sentence level. My editing philosophy dictates that unless I am removing a word from every line and a line from every paragraph, I am not actively editing. Exterminate duplicate modifiers, filler phrases, weak verbs, and rhythmic stutters (like repetitive possessive pronouns).
  > 
  > 5. **THE CHISEL**: For everything that is pulling the score down, provide a line-by-line breakdown showing me the current text, a sharp critique of its failure, and a "sculpted revision" example to show how the cadence and impact can be elevated.
  > 
  > If you understand these instructions, confirm by asking me for the text, and let me know what specific literary bars a piece must clear to earn a "9" or a "10" on your scale."*
