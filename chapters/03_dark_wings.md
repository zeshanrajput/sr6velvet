# Dark Wings

Under the hundred-thousand-watt daylight arrays of the Wuxing soundstage in Neo-Tokyo, the arena floor of *Astral Athletics: Idol Showdown* stretched across three acres of polished chrome obstacles, magnetized climbing lattices, and synchronized broadcast cameras. High in the acoustic rafters, fifty automated camera drones hovered like oversized beetles, tracking every metahuman leap and spell-assisted pivot for millions of viewers across the Pan-Asian Pacific feed. Tanaka Ryo stood at the western staging gantry in his unit's charcoal and silver racing silks, his pulse held at a steady sixty beats per minute while the floor transducers pumped synthetic adrenaline anthems through the reinforced decking.

The secondary transformer bank beneath the sub-floor let out a high-pitched acoustic scream, its cooling lines rupturing under the load of the high-voltage lighting grid. The main breaker tripped with an explosive crack, killing the daylight arrays in an instant and plunging the three-acre soundstage into sudden, suffocating darkness. In the confusion of shouting stage managers and rebooting security feeds, Ryo slipped through a heavy acoustic fire door into the backstage corridors, guided by the low-light filters in his retinas. He navigated the labyrinth of soundproofed access tunnels, reached the private VIP green room at the end of the hall, turned the brass latch, and pulled the heavy door shut behind him.

Luna sat in the low armchair in the far corner of the unlit room, her white silk stage silks catching the faint amber glow of an emergency exit sign above the private washroom. In the astral space near the acoustic ceiling panels, the bound watcher spirit tethered to Ryo's aura drifted in a slow, circular patrol—an icy draft that tracked his skin temperature, his blood pressure, and the cadence of his respiration, ready to ping corporate security at the first sign of physiological distress.

"You're late," Luna said, her voice stripped of the airy, manufactured brightness sold on the weekly broadcast feeds. A faint mechanical tick came from the cervical actuators in her neck as she turned her head toward him, her optical shutters clicking twice to adjust to the dim emergency lighting.

"The secondary corridor had security patrols scanning badges," Ryo said. The syllables slipped out in the soft, breathy cadence of Lee Ji-yoo before he caught himself, forcing his larynx down into Tanaka Ryo's rigid baritone. He sat on the arm of the leather couch opposite her, hands resting flat on his knees. In the absolute dark, away from the staging lights and corporate lenses, the tension in his throat held between his assigned profiles, the cartilage settling into an aching, neutral middle.

Luna raised a slender hand, her fingertips tracing the edge of her collarbone where Wuxing dermal plating met living skin, thin chrome seams splitting the porcelain surface in the red emergency glow. Across her collarbone, the corporate asset serial numbers were etched in fine silver leaf. "They adjusted the biometrics this morning," she said, her voice flat and even against the hum of the ventilation. "Lung capacity up twelve percent for the aerial choreography. My chest cavity feels hollow, like an empty tin can."

"They plane the cartilage first," he said, the softer elegance of Ji-yoo's phrasing still bleeding into the lower register. "Then the binding wash sets the tissue. It stays dry behind the ribs for the first year until the nerve endings stop firing."

"Does it ever stop?" she asked, her eyes catching the faint red strobe from the ceiling smoke detector.

"No," he said, the voice settling entirely into the raw, un-sculpted baseline. "You learn to stop checking the mirror."

Luna leaned forward, her shuttering optics fixing on his throat in the gloom. She reached across the small space, her fingertips touching the back of his hand. Her skin was warm from the staging lights, but the fingers were rigid with micro-hydraulic joints that lacked the irregular, organic tremors of native metahuman flesh. Through the physical contact, the hollow in her aura pressed against his astral senses, most of her native essence cleared out to seat reflex boosters, internal telemetry lines, and sensory processing chips.

"You have both," she whispered, her voice barely carrying above the low hum of the corridor air scrubbers. "Don't you?"

The watcher spirit drifted closer in the astral plane, testing for any emotional spike or unauthorized vocal deviation, but Ryo kept his respiration perfectly level, his posture locked in place as the heavy transformer beneath the sub-floor began to whine back to life. Emergency beacons along the wall baseboards flickered from amber to green.

"Hana," she whispered into the dark between them. "My real name is Hana."

The syllables settled into the quiet space, simple and un-sculpted by corporate patents.

"Jin-Young," he answered, his thumb briefly pressing against the warm chrome seam of her wrist.

She pulled her hand back a fraction of a second before the main circuit caught. The soundstage flooded with blinding white light, washing the red shadows from the walls in a single pulse. When the Wuxing floor manager shoved the door open with a tablet in hand, Hana was already sitting upright with her posture straight and her chin lifted, eyes wide and smiling for the broadcast lens, while Tanaka Ryo stood by the door, his face slipping effortlessly back into immaculate corporate poise.
