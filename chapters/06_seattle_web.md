# Seattle Web

From the flat gravel roof of the four-story Redmond tenement, the Wuxing needle cut a slender emerald mark against the low sulfur cloud cover over downtown Seattle. Cold rain drummed steadily against the rusted corrugated housing of an industrial roof exhaust, pooling in grease-stained puddles around Ryo's boots. He sat on the iron casing with his Transys Avalon commlink resting between his knees, its diagnostic display casting pale blue light across his wet leather riding trousers. Thirty feet below in the alleyway, the muffled hum of a pirated power transformer vibrated through the brickwork, competing with the distant wail of a Knight Errant patrol siren on the perimeter highway.

To lock the encrypted transmission against local grid sweeps, he released the active Cosmetic Control spell, letting the shaping magic drain from his facial tissue. Ribs widened with a deep, grinding ache as his chest expanded to its native volume, the compressed bone settling into its true frame. Cartilage along his jawline broadened with a heavy, wet click, the artificial symmetry of Tanaka Ryo dissolving as solid obsidian color pooled back into the outer rims of his irises. For the first time in three weeks, he breathed through the raw, un-sculpted lungs of Kim Jin-Young.

He initiated the encrypted hand-shake protocol. The signal jumped through three separate dead-letter relays across Denver, Vladivostok, and Hong Kong before locking onto an optical node in the Neo-Seoul network. The Avalon’s holo-emitter whirred, projecting a high-resolution terminal feed into the rainy dark.

The display stabilized.

Hana sat in an opulent, high-floor executive suite within the Wuxing Seoul Arcology, framed by floor-to-ceiling smart-glass looking out over the glowing neon sea of the Gangnam district. She wore a tailored white silk blazer over an emerald blouse, her dark hair swept up and secured with polished jade pins. Dermal plating along her collarbones gleamed beneath warm golden architectural lighting, the silver serial numbers polished until they caught the light like fine jewelry. Her cervical actuators were completely silent, upgraded to executive-grade hydraulic silent-runners that produced no mechanical tick.

"Jin-Young," she said, her voice rich, resonant, and entirely stripped of the breathy fragility he remembered from the soundstages. Her optical shutters cycled once, tracking the low-light feed from the Redmond roof.

"The routing is clean," he said, speaking in his unmasked baseline voice. "The courier in Tacoma cleared the credstick drop yesterday. Twelve thousand nuyen in certified scrip. It should cover the medical retainer for your cervical servos through the winter."

Hana looked directly into the camera array, her expression composed with the serene, untouchable authority of a corporate vice-president reviewing quarterly performance charts. "You shouldn't have called," she said quietly. "And you shouldn't have sent the money."

Ryo's fingers tightened on the alloy edge of the commlink casing, rain dripping from his chin onto the glass. "The courier had clean credentials. What happened?"

"Wuxing didn't intercept the drop, Jin," she said, her voice smooth and devoid of panic. "They cleared the payment through their primary banking node. Your entire courier chain in Seattle operates on their talent retention budget."

The rain seemed to grow heavier around him, washing over the gravel in cold, grey sheets.

"Mitsuhama and Wuxing signed a joint security accord three months ago," she continued, her gaze steady across the trans-Pacific link. "They aren't hunting you through the Barrens with tactical kill-teams. They promoted me to senior creative director for Pan-Asian Media. My face is projected across fifty-story holograms in every sector from Neo-Tokyo to Singapore. They gave me this suite, these upgrades, and this title because their actuarial models calculated that your emerging empathy would keep you looking back."

A cold, hollow weight opened in the center of his chest.

"Every payment you sent through Tacoma, every encrypted relay you bought from Seattle deckers, and every shadow contract you completed was logged on a Wuxing server," she said. "They used your concern as the exact breadcrumb trail to map your entire network in the sprawl. They know your safehouses, your contacts, and your street docs. The contracts to reclaim both of us under joint corporate ownership are already drafted and sitting on the board table."

Ryo sat motionless in the dark, the rain soaking through his collar, freezing against the bare skin of his neck. The quiet sanctuaries he thought he had been building—the noodles with Pavel, the tea in Tacoma with Ni Ni Xiaolu, the quiet payments sent across the Pacific—had not bought safety or distance. They had been converted into corporate telemetry, turning his first fragile attempts at human compassion into the very cage designed to drag him back.

"They're waiting for you to exhaust yourself," Hana said, her voice softening by a fraction of a degree. "When you realize you can't run far enough, they expect you to walk into the Seattle consulate and sign the joint agreement."

"Hana," he began, but the words caught in his dry throat.

"My name is Director Yueying now," she said, the polite, unbreakable corporate smile returning to her porcelain lips. "Do not transmit to this node again."

Ryo sat in the dark as the sulfur wind came off the Barrens, listening to the rain beat against the rusted sheet metal eaves. In the distance, the emerald needle of the Wuxing tower pulsed through the low clouds, cold and untouchable above the sprawl.

He stood up, wet gravel grinding beneath his boots.

He dropped the Avalon commlink onto the concrete, raised his heel, and drove the heavy riding sole down into the glass until the alloy buckled and the processors shorted in a brief shower of orange sparks. He kicked the scrap into the rain gutter, pulled his collar high against the damp wind, and took the fire escape down into the dark.
