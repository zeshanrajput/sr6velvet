# Seattle Web

From the flat gravel roof of the four-story Redmond tenement, the Wuxing needle cut a slender emerald mark against the low sulfur cloud cover over downtown Seattle. Cold rain drummed steadily against the rusted corrugated housing of an industrial roof exhaust, pooling in grease-stained puddles around his boots. He sat on the iron casing with his Transys Avalon commlink resting between his knees, its diagnostic display casting pale blue light across his wet leather riding trousers. Thirty feet below in the alleyway, the muffled hum of a pirated power transformer vibrated through the brickwork, competing with the distant, mournful wail of a Knight Errant patrol siren out on the perimeter highway.

To lock the encrypted transmission against local grid sweeps and avoid astral resonance reflections, he released the spells he was holding. The shaping magic drained from his facial tissue in a cold, prickling wave. Ribs widened with a deep, grinding ache as his chest expanded to its native volume, the compressed bone settling into its true frame with an audible creak. Cartilage along his jawline broadened with a heavy, wet click, the artificial symmetry of Tanaka Ryo dissolving as solid obsidian pigment pooled back into the outer rims of his irises. For the first time in three weeks, the metabolic burn behind his temples ceased, allowing him to pull air through the raw, un-sculpted lungs of Kim Jin-Young while sulfur-tinged damp hit his bronchial lining without corporate filtration. He wiped water from the commlink's optical pickup with the dry inner lining of his sleeve and initiated the encrypted hand-shake protocol.

The transmission signal jumped through three separate dead-letter relays across Denver, Vladivostok, and Hong Kong before locking onto a private optical node in the Neo-Seoul network. The Avalon’s holo-emitter whirred with a high-pitched acoustic whine, projecting a high-resolution terminal feed through the falling rain while light refracted through the wet air, throwing shimmering blue and gold halos across the gravel rooftop. The display stabilized into crisp, three-dimensional light. Hana sat in an opulent, high-floor executive suite within the Wuxing Seoul Arcology, framed by floor-to-ceiling smart-glass looking out over the glowing neon sea of the Gangnam district. Outside her window, fifty-story holographic billboards cast shifting crimson and violet hues across the polished marble floor. She wore a tailored white silk blazer over an emerald blouse, her dark hair swept up and secured with antique jade pins, while dermal plating along her collarbones gleamed beneath warm architectural lighting where silver asset serial numbers were polished like fine jewelry: *WUX-EXP-884-LUNA*. Her cervical actuators were completely silent, upgraded to executive-grade hydraulic silent-runners that produced no mechanical tick when she tilted her head.

"Jin-Young," she said, her voice rich, resonant, and entirely stripped of the breathy fragility he remembered from the soundstages. Her optical shutters cycled once, tracking the low-light feed from the Redmond roof.

"The routing is clean," he said, speaking in his unmasked baseline voice. "The courier in Tacoma cleared the credstick drop yesterday. Twelve thousand nuyen in certified scrip. It should cover the medical retainer for your cervical servos through the winter."

Hana looked directly into the camera array, her expression composed with the serene, untouchable authority of a corporate vice-president reviewing quarterly performance charts. "You shouldn't have called," she said quietly. "And you shouldn't have sent the money."

His fingers tightened on the alloy edge of the commlink casing, rain dripping from his chin onto the polarized glass. He leaned toward the emitter lens, holding his breath as the cold water soaked through his collar. "The courier had clean credentials from the docks," he said, keeping his baseline tone level. "What happened?"

"Wuxing didn't intercept the drop, Jin," she said, her voice smooth and devoid of panic. "They cleared the payment through their primary banking node. Your entire courier chain in Seattle operates on their talent retention budget." The rain seemed to grow heavier around him, washing over the gravel in cold, grey sheets that flattened his hair against his forehead. "Mitsuhama and Wuxing signed a joint security accord three months ago," she continued, her gaze steady across the trans-Pacific link. "They aren't hunting you through the Barrens with tactical kill-teams. They promoted me to senior creative director for Pan-Asian Media. My face is projected across fifty-story holograms in every sector from Neo-Tokyo to Singapore. They gave me this suite, these upgrades, and this title because their actuarial models calculated that your emerging empathy would keep you looking back."

A cold, hollow stillness opened in the center of his chest, aching far deeper than the physical shifting of bone. "Every payment you sent through Tacoma, every encrypted relay you bought from Seattle deckers, and every shadow contract you completed was logged on a Wuxing server," she said, her fingers tapping the arm of her white leather chair where chrome nail beds flashed in the golden lamplight. "They used your concern as the exact breadcrumb trail to map your entire network in the sprawl. They know your safehouses, your contacts, and your street docs. The contracts to reclaim both of us under joint corporate ownership are already drafted and sitting on the board table."

Jin-Young sat motionless in the dark, the rain soaking through his collar, freezing against the bare skin of his neck. Every gesture of emerging care had been logged, indexed, and converted into corporate telemetry. The megacorps commodified his empathy, turning his refusal to be a corporate product into the very snare designed to drag him back into the trainee dormitories.

"They're waiting for you to exhaust yourself," Hana said, her voice softening by a fraction of a degree, though her eyes remained locked on the camera. "When you realize you can't run far enough, they expect you to walk into the Seattle consulate and sign the joint agreement." When he opened his mouth, the syllable of her true name died in his dry throat. "My name is Director Yueying now," she said, the polite, unbreakable corporate smile returning to her porcelain lips, cold as glazed ceramic. "Do not transmit to this node again." The holo-emitter cut out with a sharp pop, plunging the roof back into the dark.

Jin-Young sat in the rain as the sulfur wind came off the Barrens, listening to the water drum against the rusted sheet metal eaves while in the distance across the bay, the emerald needle of the Wuxing tower pulsed through the low clouds, cold, immense, and untouchable above the sprawl. He stood up, wet gravel grinding beneath the soles of his boots. He dropped the Transys Avalon commlink onto the concrete, raised his heel, locked his knee, and drove the heavy riding sole down into the alloy casing until the glass shattered, the motherboard buckled, and the optical processors died in a brief shower of orange sparks. He kicked the crushed scrap into the rain gutter, pulled his collar high against the damp wind, and walked toward the fire escape. He took the metal stairs down into the dark, stepping off the surface grid and heading deep into the Seattle underground.
