# 8. Bliss

The rain over the West Seattle Night Market tasted of sulfur and wet copper, a persistent drizzle that flattened the steam rising from the four-stool noodle stall beneath the overpass. High above, the concrete deck groaned under the weight of automated freight haulers, sending periodic tremors through the rusted iron frame of the cart.

Velvet sat on the end stool, wrapped in a dark, unbranded oilskin slicker. She had adopted the persona of Mei Jing, a young Chinese woman in her early twenties carrying a sharp, street-hardened profile tailored for the Night Market. Dark street ink traced the tendons of her right hand and crept up the left side of her throat beneath the slicker's collar, the stylized line-work of the Forty-Sixers blending into older, faded gang marks. Her facial structure held a striking, dangerous symmetry, carrying a magnetic presence that commanded the small counter without the synthetic polish of a corporate idol. Residual warmth from the localized tissue sculpting still pulsed beneath her cheekbones, her dark eyes reflecting the amber neon of the rain.

On the counter next to a steaming bowl of pork-bone broth sat the cedar credstick basket left by Ni Ni Xiaolu's courier. Resting in the corner of the weave was a single unopened Bliss capsule, wrapped in the glossy yin-yang foil of the Octagon syndicate.

Velvet picked up the small plastic capsule. It was weightless and factory-sealed, smelling faintly of paraffin and synthetic poppy wax. She set it back down beside her bowl, where the plastic caught the dim yellow glare of the overhead bulb—a cold, fifteen-nuyen dose of quiet meant to numb a worker's spine until morning shift.

Across the counter, the old street cook lowered a basket of wheat noodles into the boiling broth. Star anise, marrow, and scallions cut through the damp tang of the street, filling the narrow space beneath the blue vinyl awning with a dense, un-engineered heat.

Footsteps splashed through the black puddle at the edge of the awning.

The man who stumbled up to the cart wore a heavy, waterlogged canvas jacket. The Vory patch on the left shoulder had been ripped away, leaving jagged gray threads hanging from the seam. He was drenched to the skin, his jaw tight, his shoulders drawn up against a cold that had nothing to do with the Seattle drizzle.

He sat two stools down without looking at Velvet. His hands, raw and trembling, gripped the edge of the cedar counter.

"Broth," the man rasped. His voice was ruined, choked with the dry, chemical cough of someone recovering from a heavy, paralyzing dose of Narcoject. "Hot."

The cook did not look up from his pot. He ladled soup into a heavy ceramic bowl and slid it across the wood.

The man reached for the wooden chopsticks, but his fingers slipped. The bamboo sticks clattered against the counter. He stared at his hands. His fingers were pale, bruised around the quick, and still twitching from the nerve-blocker Velvet had injected into his neck thirty-six hours ago inside the geothermal plant.

He turned his head slowly, his eyes bloodshot, tracking the quiet figure sitting two stools away.

Recognition hit him like a physical blow. The man’s breath hitched. His gaze locked onto the dark street ink along her neck and the cold, unmistakable focus of her eyes, recognizing the operative who had broken through his wards in the facility. His right hand twitched toward the empty holster under his arm before remembering that his sidearm lay at the bottom of a flooded utility shaft in The Oven. He froze, his knuckles locking against the wood.

It was Pavel, the Vory Sprawl Mage who had stood in the center of the illegal Bliss lab, panic in his eyes as the security wards collapsed, trying and failing to pull a spirit out of the boiling air.

"You," Pavel whispered.

Velvet did not reach for a weapon. She did not shift into a threat stance or deploy an aura mask. She simply turned her head, letting her dark gaze hold the mage’s hollow eyes.

"Eat the broth," Velvet said.

Her voice carried Mei Jing’s low, clear register, grounded with the authority of her street persona backed by the biological symmetry carved into her vocal cords. The sound anchored the narrow counter. A heavy, magnetic presence settled across the cedar wood, flattening the noise of the rain and the freight haulers above.

Pavel’s chest heaved. He swallowed hard, the dry rattle in his throat easing as the steady cadence of her voice acted on his frayed nervous system.

"I am a good mage," Pavel blurted out, his voice cracking into a desperate, shame-choked rasp. He looked down at his trembling palms. "I bound a hearth-spirit in Omsk when I was fourteen. I know the formulas. I had the circle drawn. But when I reached into the mana, it tore away from me. It choked me. The spirit wouldn't form."

He wiped his mouth with the back of his wet sleeve, his eyes wet with rain and humiliation. "I glitched. Before your team hit the room, my magic was already dead. The Vory, if they find out I choked on a simple conjuring, they won't just burn my cut. They'll drop me in the river."

Velvet looked at the trembling practitioner. In the dim neon of the market, his pale skin was slick with cold sweat, his hands still locked in a rigid tremor over the counter.

Velvet picked up her own bowl, letting the steam warm her face.

"The geothermal plant was built deep inside The Oven," Velvet said, her tone measured and objective. "Surrounded by volcanic ash, lava-charred slag, and decades of industrial dumping. The soil beneath the basement is saturated with mercury and heavy solvents. The background astral noise in that room was screaming pollution."

Pavel blinked, his trembling hands pausing against the counter.

"Your circle was clean," Velvet continued quietly, setting the bowl back down. "Your gesture was precise. The mana did not tear away from you because you were weak. It tore away because the Vory asked you to weave light in a sewer. No practitioner pulls a clean spirit out of poisoned ground."

Pavel sat motionless. His shoulders dropped three inches, the white-knuckled grip on the counter slowly easing as his breathing shifted into a long, quiet exhale. He stared down at the steam rising from his soup.

Velvet leaned slightly closer, letting the calm resonance of her voice settle directly into Pavel's awareness, steadying his pulse against the residual nerve-blocker.

"You survived the raid," Velvet said. "The lab is gone. The evidence is ash. You took a dose of Narcoject in the crossfire as a clean, painless alibi for your handlers. You were knocked out while defending the perimeter. Tell them that story."

Pavel let out a long breath. His fingers uncurled. Slowly, deliberately, he picked up the bamboo chopsticks, his grip steady now, and pulled a tangle of noodles from the broth.

Velvet stood up from the stool. She pulled two certified credsticks from her coat pocket and laid them on the wooden counter next to the cook's tray, covering Pavel's soup, her own bowl, and a tip for the quiet night.

Beside the credsticks, Velvet placed the unopened Bliss capsule. The yin-yang foil caught the dim yellow glare of the awning.

"Keep the capsule," Velvet said to the street cook. "Or throw it in the river."

Velvet pulled the hood of her oilskin slicker over her dark hair, stepped out from under the vinyl awning, and dissolved into the wet, sulfur-scented darkness of the Seattle rain.
