Sabotage

The sub-floor dressing room beneath the Singapore mega-dome sat forty feet below the coastal water table, encased in six-foot slabs of vibration-dampened ferrocrete. Outside the climate envelope, the heavy equatorial heat of the Malacca Straits pressed against the reclaimed shoreline, but down in the prep trenches, the air was chilled to a dry, sterile eighteen degrees. Through the reinforced observation viewport overlooking the staging gantry, Tanaka Ryo watched two Mitsuhama corporate mages secure an inductive copper collar around Hana’s neck. The technicians worked in silence, their charcoal coats bearing the crimson triple-rhombus of Mitsuhama Media Logistics. Their sensor gloves glowed pale emerald as they calibrated the resonance couplings, tuning the frequencies until the secondary stage conduit hummed with a low, sub-audible vibration that rattled loose bolts in the deck plates.

Hana stood motionless on the prep riser under flickering mercury vapor tubes. Her porcelain dermal seams caught the harsh downlight, throwing sharp reflections across the bare concrete walls. Her chin stayed angled toward the overhead acoustic baffle, cervical actuators clicking at two-second intervals as internal micro-fans vented heat from her vocal processors. Across her collarbones, the corporate serial numbers were inlaid in microscopic silver foil: MCI-EXP-884-LUNA.

Beside Ryo’s vanity mirror, the stage logistics feed scrolled in amber characters across the glass: Unit 7 // Broadcast Apex // Full Resonance Extraction // Terminal Encore.

“Sixty seconds to lift locks,” the floor manager’s voice came through his earpiece, flat and clipped against the muffled seismic rumble overhead. “Ten-No-Koe choreography engages on the downbeat. Clear the gantry.”

Ryo stepped away from the glass. In the vanity mirror, Tanaka Ryo stared back: charcoal tunic tailored to the millimeter with silver silk piping, shoulders broadened by structural bio-sculpting, dark hair parted with razor-sharp corporate geometry. Beneath the immaculate exterior, his larynx carried the dull, grinding ache of the vocal shift, the cartilage held rigid against the instinct to soften into Lee Ji-yoo’s lighter cadence. He checked the magnetic seals on his stage cuffs. High above his left shoulder in the astral space, the bound watcher spirit hovered—a cold draft that tracked his core temperature, his blood pressure, and the cadence of his pulse, reporting every physiological deviation straight to the security node in Kyoto.

“Ten seconds,” the floor manager said.

Ryo walked out onto the central staging platform. The copper conductor plate under his boots was chilled from the subterranean cooling coils, etched with concentric geometric spirals that channeled directly into the primary geomantic lines running beneath the arena foundation. Ji-Hoon stood at the northern riser, his chrome forearm casings gleaming under the staging floods. Tae-Hyun adjusted his lacquered wolf mask at the eastern mark, while Min-Ki checked his footing on the western boundary plate. None of them spoke. Speech outside the designated broadcast telemetry carried automatic behavioral penalty deductions against their quarterly talent trusts.

The lift engaged with a heavy hydraulic hiss. The sub-floor acoustic drivers pumped seventy tons of sound pressure through the alloy platform, vibrating straight through his shins into his jaw. The lift breached the stage deck, rising through the floor into the blinding glare of the daylight arrays. Eighty-five thousand voices hit him like a physical wall. The crowd packed the tiered terraces beneath the smart-glass dome, a vast sea of synchronized glow-sticks and flashing AR banners undulating under the humid equatorial haze. Mitsuhama Media corporate holograms fifty feet high rotated in the stadium airspace, projecting the idol unit’s faces into the clouds.

The downbeat dropped. The choreography seized the unit with automated precision. Ji-Hoon hit the northern apex, limbs snapping into position as his internal cybernetics hummed in sync with the heavy synth-percussion. Tae-Hyun drove the eastern harmony, his wolf mask throwing sharp blue shadows across the first twenty rows, while Min-Ki anchored the western perimeter. Ryo crossed the center mark on count four, boots planting on the central conductor plate as the opening vocal line began.

The circuit closed. Eighty-five thousand minds surged into the channel. The sensation struck like a river of liquid fire. The geomantic siphon opened wide across the arena floor, harvesting the raw, uncritical adoration of eighty-five thousand spectators and funneling that emotional tidal wave straight through the stage risers. As the unit’s lead channel, Ryo became the biological lightning rod. The manufactured euphoria tore through his meridians with terrifying heat, expanding his aura until the spirit ribbons of his Musok training frayed under the sheer volume of collective desire. His voice rose into the arena’s quad-directional acoustic array, pure and effortless, riding the crest of the chorus while the telemetry monitors in his peripheral vision spiked deep into the red.

Through the white glare of the pyro flares, the secondary lift crested the stage floor. Hana rose on the sacrificial riser, framed in shimmering white brocade. The instant the staging conduit touched her copper collar, her cervical actuators locked with a sharp metallic snap. Her jaw was pulled upward, throat exposed to the daylight arrays as the Mitsuhama extraction protocol began tearing into her spiritual baseline. The crowd roared in response, mistaking her rigid, trembling posture and the radiant aura flare around her shoulders for an advanced holographic effect. The collective adoration of eighty-five thousand fans, channeled through Ryo’s voice, was being compressed and focused into her neck collar, stripping her essence to charge the corporate geomantic batteries buried in the arena foundation.

Ryo hit his turnaround at bar sixteen, spinning toward the center riser. Through the swirling stage smoke and strobes, he fixed on the porcelain seam along her cheek. Her breathing came in short, shallow gasps. Her eyes were wide, the optical shutters cycling erratically as the circuit burned through her neural firmware.

Then her gaze shifted, cutting through the sweep of automated broadcast drones, and locked directly onto his face.

The rehearsed stage-smile dropped from her lips. In the two seconds before the chorus peaked, her mouth formed a quiet, crooked curve—an un-scripted expression of recognition that answered his silence and released him from his marks.

Holding his position for eight more bars would allow the extraction to complete cleanly, leaving Hana an empty shell in the medical ward while Tanaka Ryo returned to his penthouse with his corporate contract intact. The logistics division would file the incident as an acute resonance burnout during a historic performance, paying out a modest survivor stipend to her registered relatives in Neo-Seoul.

At the crescendo, as eighty-five thousand voices crested in a synchronized roar, Ryo broke alignment. He dropped his chin, clamped his teeth until his jaw joints ground together, and wrenched the flow inward. He planted his heels hard against the copper plate, twisting his ankles to short-circuit the grounding trace, and routed eighty-five thousand channels of raw, un-buffered emotional energy straight into his own bone and adept channels, severing the feed to the stage capacitors.

The backlash struck like an iron slab through his skull. His spirit conduits surged against the flood, spirit ribbons violently untangling as the sheer volume of un-filtered mana overloaded his nervous system. The inductive copper plate beneath his boots superheated, the alloy warping with a screech of shearing rivets. The synthetic leather of his boots melted into smoldering resin against his soles. White sparks showered the front rows. Along the perimeter of the dome, the geomantic storage capacitors detonated in a sequence of deafening concussions, tearing structural pylons from their concrete footings and blowing fifty-foot sheets of flame into the access corridors.

The arena transformers screamed and blew. The Singapore mega-dome plunged into total, suffocating darkness. The music died mid-note, replaced by the sound of tearing speakers and eighty-five thousand voices collapsing from synthetic euphoria into blind, stampeding panic under the shattered ceiling.

Ryo hit the smoking stage deck. The electrical surge tore through his chest, locking his lungs in a violent spasm while the acrid stench of scorched insulation and vaporized circuit boards filled the air. Red emergency beacons flickered to life along the catwalks, painting the rising smoke in bloody streaks. Through the haze, corp-sec alarms began their rhythmic wail. Tactical mages in the wings shouted over dead radio channels, scrambling to contain the crowd as the smart-glass dome above began to crack under the acoustic shockwave.

He rolled off the shattered riser, landing hard in the debris-filled service trench beneath the stage platform. Blistered fingers dragged across jagged concrete until they hooked the iron ring of a drainage hatch. He hauled the iron cover back with a guttural heave and dropped into the dark.

He plunged into waist-deep brackish seawater and industrial runoff in the storm culvert beneath the arena foundation. The cold hit his scorched skin like a sheet of needles. In the pitch blackness of the sewer, Ryo ripped the smoking Mitsuhama tunic from his shoulders, tearing the embroidered silver piping and letting the oil-slicked water wash away the stage foundation and grey streaks of sweat from his neck. Above him, the rumble of the panicked stadium faded into the distant drone of harbor sirens.

The bound watcher spirit was gone, incinerated in the geomantic backlash. His commlink lay dead in the sludge. Wading against the tide of seawater, hands stinging from fresh electrical burns across his palms, Ryo pushed through the subterranean channels toward the container docks at Jurong—nameless, stripped of his corporate skin, and stepping into the shadows on his own terms.
