# Bliss

Beneath the West Seattle elevated freight overpass, automated cargo haulers rumbled across steel expansion joints every sixty seconds, sending a deep shudder through the rusted framework of the roadside noodle cart. Cold rain dripped from the torn vinyl awning into the curb drain, hissing against the cast-iron burner where forty gallons of rich pork-bone broth simmered in a heavy aluminum cauldron. Velvet sat on the corner wooden stool, wrapped in a dark oilskin slicker, wearing the street persona of Mei Jing. Faded blue syndicate ink traced the tendons of her right wrist and rose along the left side of her jaw beneath the upturned canvas collar.

On the cedar counter next to her steaming bowl sat the credstick basket from the Octagon Triad courier. Resting in the corner of the weave was a single unopened Bliss capsule wrapped in silver yin-yang foil, fifteen nuyen of synthetic chemical numbness meant as a gratuity for the courier's run. She picked up the small cylinder, turning the plastic between her bare fingers before setting it back beside the broth. Across the counter, the elderly cook lowered fresh wheat noodles into boiling water with a wire basket, the rising steam creating a warm, clouded pocket of air that held the rain at bay.

Water splashed in the gutter as heavy boots dragged along the pavement. A man stumbled into the shelter of the awning, wearing a waterlogged canvas work jacket where the Russian Vory insignia on the left shoulder had been violently hacked off with a combat blade, leaving frayed grey threads hanging over torn ballistic weave. He slumped onto the stool two seats away, his raw, bruised hands trembling against the wooden counter as he fought to steady his breathing.

"Broth," the man rasped, his vocal cords raw from the aftereffects of inhaled aerosol sedatives. "Hot."

The cook slid a heavy ceramic bowl across the cedar wood without asking for creds up front. The man reached for a pair of bamboo chopsticks from the stoneware jar, but his twitching fingers lost their grip, sending the sticks clattering across the wet laminate. He stared down at his swollen knuckles, his forearms still suffering involuntary tremors from the electrical nerve-blocker Velvet had driven into his cervical cluster thirty-six hours earlier during the geothermal plant breach in Puyallup.

He turned his head slowly, bloodshot eyes fixing on the woman sitting two stools away.

His breath hitched in his throat as he recognized the faded ink along her jawline and the unnerving, motionless calm in her dark eyes. His right hand jerked instinctively toward an empty armpit holster before remembering that his heavy Ceska submachine gun was currently buried beneath ten feet of toxic sludge in the flooded plant sump. The man was Pavel, the young Vory combat mage who had broken under fire as the perimeter wards collapsed around the illicit narcotics lab.

"You," Pavel whispered, his voice trembling as he pressed his back hard against the cart's support pole.

Velvet did not reach for a weapon, nor did her posture tighten into a combat stance. She held his gaze across the steaming counter with quiet, level stillness, her hands resting open beside her ceramic bowl.

"Drink the broth," she said, her voice low, steady, and carrying the soothing cadence of Lee Ji-yoo. "The chili oil clears the chemical residue from the lungs."

Pavel swallowed hard, his chest heaving under the damp canvas jacket as he looked between her open hands and the steaming soup. "I know the containment formulas," he blurted out, the words spilling past his lips in a rush of shame and exhausted panic. "I bound a hearth-spirit when I was fourteen in Vladivostok. I had the chalk circle drawn on the concrete. But the mana tore away the second I spoke the binding mantra. The spirit wouldn't take form."

He wiped his mouth with a soaked sleeve, his eyes bright with the terror of a syndicate soldier awaiting execution. "I froze. Before your strike team breached the iron doors, my magic was already dead in my hands. If the Vory elders think I choked on a basic third-circle summon, they'll weigh my boots with scrap iron and dump me in the Duwamish river before sunrise."

Velvet looked at the young mage, his skin grey and hollow under the single yellow work bulb hanging from the cart awning.

She picked up her bowl, letting the ceramic heat soak into her palms. "The geothermal plant was built directly over the Puyallup ash beds," Velvet said quietly. "Surrounded by subterranean coal fires, heavy industrial slag, and un-filtered chemical runoff from the old refineries. The ground water beneath the floor was saturated with industrial mercury. The local background count was screaming pollution at a level four severity."

Pavel blinked, his trembling hands freezing over the wooden counter as the technical explanation registered.

"Your chalk circle was structurally intact," Velvet continued, her tone matter-of-fact and calm. "Your vocal gesture was clean. The mana broke because the earth beneath your boots was poisoned. No mage in Seattle summons a clean hearth-spirit out of industrial sewage without suffering severe drain backlash."

Pavel sat completely motionless on the stool. His shoulders slowly sank, the white-knuckled grip on his knees relaxing as he let out a long, ragged breath that mixed with the rising steam.

"You took a stray sedative dart during the initial breach," Velvet added, her dark eyes meeting his with steady assurance. "You were knocked unconscious while holding the northern access corridor. Tell your brigadier that story, and keep your head down until the heat clears."

Pavel looked down at his hands, finding them steady for the first time in two days. He picked up a fresh pair of chopsticks from the jar, dipped them into the dark broth, and pulled noodles to his mouth.

They sat together beneath the rattling awning as the rain poured over the West Seattle overpass, eating hot noodles in the quiet shelter away from the sprawl, leaving the silver Bliss capsule untouched in the credstick basket.
