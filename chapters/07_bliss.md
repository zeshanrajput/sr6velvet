# Bliss

Beneath the West Seattle elevated freight overpass, automated Ares Macrotechnology cargo haulers rumbled across steel expansion joints every sixty seconds, sending a deep shudder through the rusted framework of the roadside noodle cart. Cold rain dripped from the torn vinyl awning into the curb drain, hissing against the cast-iron burner where forty gallons of rich pork-bone broth simmered in a heavy aluminum cauldron. In the wet air, holographic AR banners from Shiawase Logistics and Yakashima Food Services flickered and glitched across the concrete pillars, casting jagged amber reflections in oil-slicked puddles. Velvet sat on the corner wooden stool, wrapped in a dark oilskin slicker, wearing the street persona of Mei Jing. Faded blue syndicate ink traced the tendons of her right wrist and rose along the left side of her jaw beneath the upturned canvas collar, anchoring her physical presence in the dockside working class.

On the cedar counter next to her steaming bowl sat the credstick basket from the Octagon Triad courier, holding in the corner of its weave a single unopened Bliss capsule wrapped in silver yin-yang foil—fifteen nuyen of synthetic chemical numbness meant as a gratuity for the courier's run. She picked up the small cylinder, turning the plastic between her bare fingers before setting it back beside the broth while across the counter. The elderly cook lowered fresh wheat noodles into boiling water with a wire basket, the rising steam creating a warm, clouded pocket of air that held the sulfur rain at bay.

Water splashed in the gutter as heavy boots dragged along the broken asphalt, and a man stumbled into the shelter of the awning wearing a waterlogged Ares work jacket where the Russian Vory insignia on the left shoulder had been violently hacked off with a combat blade, leaving frayed grey threads hanging over torn ballistic weave.

He slumped onto the stool two seats away, raw, bruised hands trembling against the wooden counter as he fought to steady his respiration. "Broth," the man rasped, his vocal cords raw from the aftereffects of inhaled aerosol sedatives. "Hot." The cook slid a heavy ceramic bowl across the cedar wood without asking for creds up front, but when the man reached for bamboo chopsticks from the stoneware jar, his twitching fingers lost their grip, sending the sticks clattering across the wet laminate. He stared down at his swollen knuckles, his forearms still suffering involuntary tremors from the Narcoject autoinjector Velvet had driven into his cervical cluster thirty-six hours earlier. When he turned his head slowly, bloodshot eyes fixed on the woman sitting two stools away, his breath caught in his throat as he recognized the faded triad ink along her jawline and the unnerving, motionless calm in her dark eyes. His right hand jerked instinctively toward an empty armpit holster, his eyes wide with terror.

The man was Pavel, the young Vory combat mage who had broken under fire as the perimeter wards collapsed around the illicit narcotics refinery. "You," Pavel whispered, his voice trembling as he pressed his spine hard against the cart's iron support pole.

Velvet kept her hands open beside her ceramic bowl, meeting his panic with quiet, grounded stillness. "Drink the broth," she said, her voice low, steady, and carrying the soothing, resonant cadence of Lee Ji-yoo. "The chili oil clears the chemical residue from the bronchial lining."

Pavel swallowed hard, his chest heaving under the damp canvas jacket as he looked between her open hands and the steaming soup. "I know the containment formulas," he blurted out, the words spilling past his lips in a rush of shame and exhausted panic. "I bound a hearth-spirit when I was fourteen in Vladivostok. I had the chalk circle drawn on the concrete. But the mana tore away the second I spoke the binding mantra. The spirit wouldn't take form. Before your strike team breached the iron doors, my magic was already dead in my hands. If the Vory elders think I choked on a basic third-circle summon, they'll weigh my boots with scrap iron and dump me in the Duwamish river before sunrise."

Velvet looked at the young mage, his skin grey and hollow under the single yellow work bulb hanging from the cart awning. She picked up her bowl, letting the ceramic heat soak into her palms. "The geothermal plant was built directly over the Puyallup ash beds," Velvet said quietly, her tone matter-of-fact and calm. "Surrounded by subterranean coal fires, heavy industrial slag, and un-filtered chemical runoff from the old Renraku smelters, the ground water beneath the floor was saturated with industrial mercury. The local background count was screaming toxic pollution. Your chalk circle was structurally intact and your vocal gesture was clean, but the mana broke because the earth beneath your boots was poisoned. No hermetic mage in Seattle summons a clean spirit out of industrial sewage. You took a sedative dart during the initial breach and lost consciousness while holding the northern access corridor. Tell that story, and keep your head down."

Pavel sat completely motionless on the stool as the white-knuckled grip on his knees relaxed, letting out a long, ragged breath that mixed with the rising steam from the aluminum cauldron. He looked down at his hands, finding them steady for the first time in two days, then picked up a fresh pair of chopsticks from the jar, dipped them into the dark broth, and pulled noodles to his mouth. They sat together beneath the rattling awning as the rain poured over the West Seattle overpass, eating hot noodles in the quiet shelter away from the sprawl, leaving the silver Bliss capsule untouched in the credstick basket.
